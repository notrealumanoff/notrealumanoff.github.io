History of Mechanical Clocks

The first mechanical clocks were astronomical clocks and tower clocks with verge escapements from around the 1300s. These clocks were not very accurate, and the driving force from the weight powering the clock affected the clock's speed. In 1656, Christiaan Huygens, a Dutch engineer and mathematician, invented the first pendulum clock. Although it still used a verge escapement, the pendulum, which had an adjustable length, almost entirely prevented the issue of driving force speeding up or slowing down the clock. Huygens built the pendulum clock with clockmaker Salomon Coster, reducing timekeeping errors from 15 minutes a day to 15 seconds a day.


How Long were Mechanical Clocks the Most Accurate Clocks for?

The mechanical clock was the most accurate type of clock from the 1300s until the 1930s, when electric clocks became more accurate and more affordable than mechanical clocks.
