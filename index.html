History of Mechanical Clocks

The first mechanical clocks were astronomical clocks and tower clocks with verge and foliot escapements, which used an oscillating inertial weight from around the 1300s. These clocks were not very accurate, and the energy loss from the friction in the movement affected the speed of the clock. In December 1656, Christiaan Huygens (1629-1695), a Dutch engineer and mathematician, invented the first pendulum clock. Although it still used a verge escapement, it also used a pendulum with an adjustable length, which almost entirely prevented energy loss in the clock movement. Huygens built the pendulum clock with clockmaker Salomon Coster (c.1620-1659) in 1657, reducing timekeeping errors from 15 minutes a day to 15 seconds a day. These early clocks used a single hand, the hour hand, and analogue clocks with both an hour and a minute hand became common in the late 1700s and early 1800s.


For How Long were Mechanical Clocks the Most Accurate Clocks?

The mechanical clock was the most accurate type of clock from the 1300s until the 1930s, when electric clocks became more accurate and more affordable than mechanical clocks.


The first pendulum clock designed by Christiaan Huygens in 1656 and built by Salomon Coster in 1657 in The Hague, Netherlands. It uses a pendulum to drive the clock, which drastically improved timekeeping at the time.


The Prague Astronomical Clock (Czech: Pražský orloj) in Prague, Czechia, was completed around 1410 by Mikuláš of Kadaň (1350-1419) and Jan Šindel (1370s-between 1455 and 1457). This building, which is also the old Prague Town Hall, has 7 clock faces (a four-faced tower clock, an astronomical clock, and two other clock faces on the building). It also has a calendar dial below the astronomical clock dial.


The Peacock Clock (Russian: Павлин часы) is a massive 18th-century gilded automaton built in the 2nd half of the 18th century by James Cox (c.1723-1800) and acquired by Catherine the Great in 1781. The clock chimes music and sets three life-sized mechanical birds into motion in a specific sequence - an owl, a peacock, and a rooster.


The most famous mechanical clock, the Elizabeth Tower Clock, was built by Edward John Dent (1790-1853) and Frederick Dent (c.1814-1860) in England, and completed in 1859, chiming the famous Westminster chimes. The clock is commonly referred to as 'Big Ben' by many people, although 'Big Ben' is the name of the largest bell in the clock tower, not the clock tower itself.
